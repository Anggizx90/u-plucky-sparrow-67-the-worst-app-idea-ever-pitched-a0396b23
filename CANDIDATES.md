# Candidate pitches — The Worst App Idea Ever Pitched

Judge: z-ai/glm-5.3-flash, pairwise. Criteria in order:
1. Names a specific app and what it uselessly does, in one sentence pitched as the worst idea imaginable.
2. Makes the uselessness funny through absurd logic, deadpan confidence, or a perfect mismatch between a problem nobody has and the app that solves it.
3. Sounds like a real pitch, with a product name or a clear statement of what the app does and who it is for.
4. Uses the 240 characters well — one clean sentence, no padding, no second idea crowding it.
5. Leaves a vivid, specific picture of the app in the reader's head rather than a generic joke about apps.

Scores are per-criterion (1–5), in criterion order. All 24 drafts are single sentences.

## Head-to-head refinement (top 3 + 2 challengers, expanded to use the 240 chars)

- **A. Lowbatt (203):** "Lowbatt is an app that warns you your phone battery is dying by draining your phone battery, because the most honest warning is the one that causes the very problem it is warning you about, in real time." — 5, 5, 4, 4, 4. Purest self-defeating logic; weakest on naming who it is for.
- **B. QueuePal (216):** "QueuePal lets you hire a stranger to stand in line for you at a store that has no line, then livestreams the empty queue to your phone so you can watch your precious time being saved, one unoccupied minute at a time." — 5, 5, 4, 4, 5. Two stacked jokes (hire for a nonexistent line + livestream of nothing); most vivid picture.
- **C. Toastd (213):** "Toastd is an app that watches your toast brown in real time and alerts you the exact second it becomes toast, for busy professionals who refuse to trust the eyes they have already used, free of charge, since 1919." — 5, 5, 5, 4, 4. Best named audience; the app at least works, so slightly less "worst" than A/B.
- **D. Elevatr (204):** "Elevatr is a social network exclusively for people currently riding in elevators, where you can chat with strangers you will never see again for exactly four floors, then say goodbye forever at the lobby." — 5, 4, 5, 4, 5. Cute, but the C2 punch is softer than A/B/C.
- **E. FridgeLight (187):** "FridgeLight is an app that tells you whether your fridge light is on with the door closed, finally solving the one great mystery humanity collectively agreed to stop caring about in 1952." — 5, 5, 4, 3, 4. Great deadpan line, but shorter and no named audience.

## Winner

**B, refined to 228 chars** — adds the named audience ("busy people") and tightens the
phrasing, using 228 of the 240 characters with no padding and no second idea:

> QueuePal lets busy people hire a stranger to stand in line for them at a store that has no line, then livestreams the empty queue to their phone so they can watch their precious time being saved, one unoccupied minute at a time.

Why it wins head-to-head: on C1 all finalists tie (specific app + useless function, one sentence).
On C2, QueuePal beats Toastd/Elevatr/FridgeLight because it stacks two absurdities — paying a
stranger to queue where there is no queue, then watching the emptiness on a livestream — and it
beats Lowbatt because the mismatch (a problem nobody has, solved with maximum ceremony) is the
judge's own example of funny uselessness, while Lowbatt's self-harm is a single paradox. On C3
the refined version names its audience; on C4 it uses 228/240 cleanly; on C5 the livestreamed
empty queue is the most specific, most filmable image in the field.
